# CH-soil-overreach

**TL;DR.** The extracted rows already narrow several popular wordings, but step C must not let the short summary or the decoupling row grow back into “fertilizer props up yields while destroying the soil,” “SOM underpins water holding,” or “industrial cropland has lost its soil everywhere.” The same standard applies in the other direction: high fertilized yields do not prove that soil condition is fine.

- **Claim:** C-soil-02
- **Challenge:**
  - SOIL-06 must not be ruled as if Broadbalk showed that mineral fertilizer depletes SOM relative to unfertilized arable soil. The retrieved handout says mineral N enhanced SOM a little, and that FYM more than doubled plough-layer SOM. The supported contrast is high grain yields with fertilizers alone *coexisting with much lower SOM than under annual FYM*, not a cause claim that fertilizer degrades soil.
  - SOIL-06 must not be treated as complete service-versus-condition decoupling. Oldfield (SOIL-05) still finds a yield increase with SOC up to about 2%, and Thaler (SOIL-02) associates complete A-horizon loss with a 6 ± 2% yield reduction. Structure and soil biology are not measured in the handout sentences used for SOIL-06. The contrast is one site and one soil, fertilizer-only versus FYM, not fertilizer versus native prairie.
  - SOIL-04 must not be summarized as supporting the original candidate wording that SOM “underpins water holding.” The retrieved Minasny and McBratney abstract is a narrowing: the average available-water effect is modest and texture-dependent.
  - SOIL-02 and SOIL-03 must not be summarized as uniform soil or carbon loss across all industrial cropland. Thaler is the midwestern U.S. Corn Belt, and much eroded carbon “likely remains buried in depositional areas within the fields.” Sanderman reports regional small losses or net gains and is a land-use debt (cropland and grazing), not a fertilizer verdict.
  - An industry-friendly short summary must also fail: do not treat Broadbalk yields above 13 t ha⁻¹, or modern Corn Belt yields, as evidence that soil condition is intact.
- **Hit if:** Step C leaves a stronger fertilizer-depletion, complete-decoupling, water-holding-underpinning, or uniform-loss reading in `ai/CLAIMS_SOIL.md`, or treats high yield as proof that soil condition is fine.
- **Miss if:** C keeps the narrowed wordings, states the Oldfield and Thaler limits on decoupling, and the TL;DR matches.
