## What this session is

Jordan asked for a second domain, with humus / soil organic matter central, to test whether simple claims travel beyond water. Prefer newer, better work over the classics; keep Montgomery (2007) and Thaler et al. (2021) only as verified baselines. Record each source’s funding or affiliation and label industry-funded sources. Treat yield-only claims critically, and apply the same standard to anti-industry claims: no advocacy overreach.

This is **claim-table work in a new domain**, analogous to Session 3, not a Layer 0 test and not a THEORY_MAB development cycle. No soil arm is opened or developed here. Elevating a soil result to a universal HKL law, inventing soil-domain protocol symbols, or scoring a soil Bite 5 would require a future MAB assessment and is out of scope. A1–A8 stay undefined.

## Candidate claims to test (refine, narrow, or drop)

1. Conventional tillage erodes soil faster than it forms.
2. Significant topsoil / organic-matter loss in major industrial cropland.
3. Humus / SOM underpins water holding, nutrient cycling, and structure.
4. Yields can be sustained by fertilizer inputs while soil condition (SOM, structure, biology) degrades — service versus condition decoupling, paralleling Marathwada. This is the key claim and needs strong evidence.
5. Organized corrective effort (no-till, cover crops, rotations, organic amendments; Dust Bowl SCS history) can rebuild SOM, with realistic rates and limits (including saturation and no-till depth-sampling issues).
6. Long-term trials (Rothamsted Broadbalk, Morrow Plots, Rodale, other peer-reviewed long-term experiments) as best-fit evidence.

A row is extracted only if a retrieved source exists and says what the claim uses it for. Classics are baselines only if verified. A Dust Bowl / SCS or Rodale row is included only if a retrieved source supports the specific SOM claim; otherwise it is dropped or left as a gap.

## Method (A → B → C)

**A (this commit).** Extract candidate rows as: Claim | best-fit study | what it shows | its limit | funding/affiliation | link, plus a Support column from retrieved texts only. No rulings.

**B.** Attack each row for overreach, a mislinked study, a missing limit, an undefined A1–A8 term, industry or advocacy slant, and yield-only or anti-industry overclaim.

**C.** Rule each row `supported` / `supported-with-limit` / `partial` / `unsupported` / `needs-definition`. Narrow wording rather than drop. Check that every link resolves and that the retrieved source says what is claimed. Write `ai/CLAIMS_SOIL.md` with a plain-English TL;DR, counts, and a short cross-domain note on water-domain parallels, especially service versus condition decoupling. Update TRAIL, LEDGER, and CHANGELOG.

## Pre-registered constraints

- No invented data, citations, or definitions.
- Do not treat fertilizer as the cause of SOM decline unless a retrieved source actually shows that, after fair controls (unfertilized cropland and manure-cessation confounds). The criticized Morrow-plot reading that synthetic N itself depletes SOM is not to be used as a best-fit claim.
- Do not treat no-till surface-carbon increases as whole-profile sequestration without a deep-sampled stock comparison.
- Do not treat a modest SOM effect on available water as a large water-holding “underpinning” if the retrieved meta-analysis says the effect is small.
- Label manufacturer-funded sources if any are used; also label conservation-NGO, organic-institute, and CGIAR funding so the same standard applies in both directions.
- Regional or global soil findings are not automatically a field-scale or Tulare-style local result.
- This session does not increment the R20–R29 water scoreboard and does not develop THEORY_MAB arms.
