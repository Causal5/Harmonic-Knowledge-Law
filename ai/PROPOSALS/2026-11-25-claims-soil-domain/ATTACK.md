## Overreach, both directions

1. **SOIL-06 is the key row and must stay a coexistence claim, not a cause claim.** The official Broadbalk handout supports high grain yields (now exceeding 13 t ha⁻¹) with fertilizers only *or* with FYM plus extra N, while long-term FYM more than doubled plough-layer SOM and mineral N only enhanced SOM a little. That is not “fertilizer depletes SOM,” and it is not “yields prove the soil is fine.” An anti-industry short summary and an industry-messaging short summary would both overreach.

2. **Decoupling is incomplete.** Oldfield et al. (2019) find that maize and wheat yields still rise with SOC up to about 2%, after accounting for N fertilizer rate. Thaler et al. (2021) associate complete A-horizon loss with a 6 ± 2% yield reduction. C should rule SOIL-06 no stronger than `supported-with-limit`, and should say why it is not complete decoupling.

3. **SOIL-04 narrows, rather than supports, “SOM underpins water holding.”** The retrieved abstract reports about 1.16 mm of available water per 100 mm of soil for a 1% mass increase in OC, larger in sands and smaller in clays. Do not put the original candidate wording back into the TL;DR.

4. **Loss is regional, not uniform.** Thaler is the U.S. Corn Belt, with in-field deposition of eroded carbon. Sanderman’s 133 Pg C debt is a modeled land-use counterfactual to 2 m, with some agricultural zones showing small losses or net gains, and it is not a fertilizer verdict.

5. **Montgomery (2007) is a verified classic baseline for erosion versus production, not an SOM study and not the preferred industrial-cropland source.** Thaler is the newer, better regional row for (2).

## Funding and advocacy, both directions

6. **No best-fit study in this table is agrochemical-manufacturer funded.** That fact should be stated once. It is not a search of the industry literature, and it is not a license to treat every row as an anti-industry result.

7. **Conservation-NGO and foundation funding must be labeled:** The Nature Conservancy and the Doris Duke Charitable Foundation on Sanderman; Wood’s Nature Conservancy co-affiliation on Oldfield. CGIAR CCAFS funding on Powlson must be labeled too.

8. **Powlson (2014) and Poulton (2018) are public-research limits, not industry talking points.** Do not convert “no overall stock increase in 69 pairs sampled to ≥40 cm” into “no-till does not work,” or “severe limitations to 4 per 1000 over large areas” into “corrective effort cannot raise SOM.” Early FYM rates in Poulton were high; they moved toward equilibrium and could reverse.

9. **Do not restore dropped wordings.** Khan et al. (2007) is not a best-fit. Rodale is not a retrieved full-text best-fit. Dust Bowl SCS is not a locked SOM-rebuild-rate study. The candidate structure claim has no extracted best-fit row.

## Missing limits and retrieval status

10. **SOIL-04** rests on the Crossref abstract, not a retrieved publisher PDF.

11. **SOIL-06** rests on an official institutional handout plus Poulton et al. (2018). One site, 0–23 cm, no separate structure or biology measurements in the cited sentences.

12. **SOIL-08** rests on the official 2014 EGU abstract of a 2015 journal meta-analysis whose PDF was not retrieved. Depth is 22 cm. “No saturation visible in the observations” is not a finding of no long-term equilibrium.

13. **SOIL-07** is a perspective synthesizing prior comparisons, not a new trial. Surface-concentration benefits remain, and conservation-agriculture erosion rates in Montgomery are a separate claim.

14. **The Marathwada parallel is analogical.** Yield (service) versus SOM (condition) can be compared with water service versus groundwater condition, but this session does not transfer protocol symbols, invent soil-domain quantities, or develop a THEORY_MAB arm. No A1–A8 term is needed to rule these rows.
