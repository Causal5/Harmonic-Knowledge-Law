# The Sahel as Worked Example

## Regulation, local correction, and scale — definition reconciliation

**Status:** Working application and test proposal, 16 October 2026.
Definitions inherit the current
[registry](../Registry/causal-ethics-master-symbol-registry.md),
[Extension](../Delta-Self/delta-self-extension.md),
[HKL](../HKL/hkl-lyapunov.md), and

## 1. Preserved research question

The original Sahel narrative compares two proposed failure mechanisms:
restriction of local adaptive options under colonial and successor-state
agricultural policy, and poor alignment between centrally specified
restoration designs and heterogeneous local conditions. It contrasts these
with farmer-managed natural regeneration and distributed local knowledge.

The research question remains: can the local match among information,
control, ecological conditions, and corrective action explain outcomes
beyond total expenditure alone?

The [earlier text](../archive/sahel_worked_example.md) preserves the full
historical narrative. Its dates, scale estimates, and causal attribution
require source-level verification before serving as matched empirical data.
The cases are not treated as a controlled before-and-after experiment merely
because they occupy a broad region or overlap in time.

## 2. Definition migration

| Earlier wording | Current interpretation |
|---|---|
| “OIE is a field” | OIE is a process; its measured burden-generation rates may be spatially distributed |
| “PAE remains a scalar budget” | PAE is a principle; planned allocation, actual expenditure, and achieved effects are separate quantities |
| $G_i$ as the model | $G_i$ is substrate; $m_i$ is the model encoded through it |
| $S_\Phi$ as an agent-accessible manifold | $S_\Phi$ is conditioned ontic constraint structure |
| $R(t)$ as all possible futures | Distinguish $\mathcal R^{ont}_\Omega(t;h)$ from the estimate $\mathcal R_i(t;h)$ |
| $V(R),V(D)$ as variety | Reserve $V$ for HKL burden; any variety measure needs separate local notation and an explicit discrimination scheme |
| $\lambda_{PAE}$ as a measured universal homomorphism efficiency | A model-specific conversion needs calibration; model adequacy may be one explanatory factor |

This revision retains the field-versus-aggregate insight: a total can conceal
where burden arises and where correction lands. It does not identify OIE
itself with a scalar or a universal physical field.

## 3. Candidate mechanisms

| Case in the source narrative | Candidate mechanism | What a test must distinguish |
|---|---|---|
| Restrictions on mobility and diverse land use | Policy changes feasible responses and local feedback | Policy effects versus rainfall, prices, institutions, and prior degradation |
| Centrally specified restoration | Model, timing, placement, or incentives fail to match local conditions | Model mismatch versus inadequate resources, implementation, or selection of harder sites |
| Farmer-managed natural regeneration | Local knowledge and incentives improve targeting and feedback | Architecture effects versus site differences, rainfall, labor, tenure, and other concurrent changes |

Ashby and Conant–Ashby motivate the regulation questions in the earlier
paper. This framework's mapping to those results is a proposal with
additional assumptions. It does not yield a calibrated efficiency or a
general theorem that centralization makes correction impossible.

## 4. Effort and landed correction

Record local planned allocations $\mathcal Q^{nom}_{PAE}$, expended effort
$E_{PAE,i,k}$, and achieved corrective rates $\dot B^{land}_{PAE,k}$.
Money, labor, water, and energy retain their own units.

Two interventions with equal budgets can have different effects. A model
may separate fidelity to the local target from physical actuation,
coordination, latency, and placement. The separable homomorphism model in
the v0.4 proposal remains uncalibrated; it is not used to infer successful
correction from expenditure.

Aggregate only after specifying channel units, spatial support, overlap,
and transfers. Report patch-level losses alongside any basin total.
Correction exported from a source patch or burden shifted to a recipient
must remain visible in the joint accounting.

## 5. Claims that require calculation

The earlier wording that $dV/dt$ “measurably flipped sign,” that scalar
accounting correctly predicted both outcomes, and that the framework
“passed” a falsification test is withdrawn as a statement of completed
analysis. No defined and calculated burden series, calibrated conversion,
or held-out comparison accompanies that text.

Ecological improvement can motivate an HKL interpretation. It is not
automatically a measurement of $V$, $\dot V$, or $\lambda_{PAE}$.
The hypotheses remain available for testing.

## 6. Bounded test

1. Specify comparable locations, observation years, interventions, and outcomes.
2. Verify primary historical and ecological sources; record uncertainty.
3. Measure effort and physical outcomes separately, including side effects.
4. Fit a baseline using relevant environmental and institutional variables.
5. Test whether declared local-regulation variables improve held-out results.
6. Only then construct a normalized burden candidate and test its incremental value.

The [logistic addendum](sahel-logistic-addendum-section9-draft.md) remains
a separate unpromoted source. Increasing its logistic parameter toward four
cannot be assumed to improve fixed-point stability. The
[definition decisions](../Registry/definition-decisions-2026-10-16.md)
record the unresolved mapping and reachability normalization.

## 7. Connection to scaling

The Sahel case motivates the
[Fallacy of Large-Scale Absorbic Effort](../Papers/fallacy-of-large-scale-absorbic-effort.md)
and [Continuation Filter](../Papers/continuation-filter.md) hypotheses.
It supplies candidate mechanisms and observable comparisons, rather than a
completed proof of their civilizational generalization.
