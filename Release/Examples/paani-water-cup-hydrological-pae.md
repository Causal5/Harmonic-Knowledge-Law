# Hydrological PAE in Maharashtra: The Paani Foundation Water Cup

Villages in Maharashtra organized ridge-to-valley conservation works and tracked groundwater levels and winter planting. The Water Cup is a worked example of the **Principle of Absorbic Effort (PAE)**[¹](#term-1). Villagers supplied the corrective effort. The earthworks changed the path rainfall could take across their fields. The Foundation scored construction and monitored what followed. [Water Cup design, §§3–5 and 10](https://www.paanifoundation.in/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Satyamev-Jayate-Water-Cup-2019-Marking-System-1.pdf) · [Project registry, §VI](../Registry/causal-ethics-master-symbol-registry.md)

## What the Water Cup measured

The 2019 competition involved 4,706 villages. The Foundation estimated that the works added 232.13 billion litres of storage capacity. That figure measures potential capacity. The separate drought-year evaluation followed the **2018 competition cohort** through May 2019. [Foundation report, competition results](https://www.paanifoundation.in/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Water-Cup-2019-Report.pdf#page=27)

The evaluation included 971 dug wells in 100 high-performing Water Cup villages and 655 in 67 geographically matched comparison villages. Rainfall records placed 96 of the 167 villages in areas more than 40% below normal rainfall. In Marathwada, participating villages retained more winter planting while their wells fell farther. In North Maharashtra, participating villages had a smaller well-level decline while retaining less planting. The Foundation proposed irrigation demand as an explanation. The comparison describes selected villages over one drought year. Some comparison villages could have received government watershed works, data checks were pending, and the tables give no metered irrigation volumes. [Foundation report, Tables 3–4 and limitations](https://www.paanifoundation.in/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Water-Cup-2019-Report.pdf#page=21)

In its subsequent Samruddha Gaon program, the Foundation recorded 18,649 water-level readings from 7,359 wells in 734 villages over roughly a year. A crop survey in 600 villages recorded area planted and irrigation method. The published summaries do not identify a matched continuation of the original Water Cup cohort. [2021–22 annual report, well measurements and crop survey](https://paanifoundation.in/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Jan-2021-Feb-2022-Annual-Report_compressed-1.pdf#page=4)

## What field studies establish about recharge

**Applied water enters soil and moves through different layers.** At three flooded orchard plots near Modesto, California, researchers sampled a bromide tracer[³](#term-3) and logged soil moisture every ten minutes. They detected the tracer at successive depths, down to five metres in the deepest instrumented profile. [Zhou and colleagues, §2.2 and Figure 6](https://www.maderacountywater.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Zhou_et_al_2023_Bowman.pdf)

**Recharge from the surface can take years to reach a deep water table.** At Oro Grande Wash, California, sensors tracked water moving down from a recharge pond. Initial arrival at the water table[⁴](#term-4), 117 metres below ground, took three years. After the intervening sediments had wetted, arrival took one year. [Izbicki, Flint and Stamos, 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6584.2007.00406.x)

**Water from a particular recharge basin can reach a particular well.** At Kraemer Basin, California, researchers traced water from the recharge pond to monitoring and production wells. They repeated the experiment in 2008 after the initial 1998 test. Travel times to most wells agreed within measurement uncertainty. [Clark and colleagues, 2014](https://doi.org/10.3390/w6061826)

The Maharashtra study documents organized corrective effort and observed village outcomes. The California studies establish infiltration, travel time and well connection at their respective sites. Estimating how much Water Cup work replenished a particular aquifer requires measurements that connect the village intervention to that aquifer. ## Glossary

<a id="term-1"></a>¹ **Principle of Absorbic Effort (PAE).** Elective corrective effort by a capable agent to absorb, prevent or locally reconcile a destabilizing burden. Here the effort is the villagers’ conservation work.

<a id="term-2"></a>² **Rabi.** The winter cropping season in Maharashtra.

<a id="term-3"></a>³ **Tracer.** An identifiable substance added to water so that sampling can show where and when the tagged water arrives.

<a id="term-4"></a>⁴ **Infiltration, recharge and water table.** Infiltration is water entering soil. Recharge is water reaching the saturated groundwater system. The water table is the upper surface of groundwater in an unconfined aquifer.
